v0.1.99

          
Mon, 19 Oct 2026

        

        
A far member's receipt walks all the way home again: a relay further down the chain keeps carrying it, a retry interval apart, until it hears the next station carry it on, so a group line from the far end reaches [all]. The two-copy cap stays for the station next to the sender. Each line copy is now logged as it goes to the modem, so retries after [ok], which show no mark, can be seen in the log.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.98

          
Sun, 19 Oct 2026

        

        
A neighbour hears a direct message on its first copy: a retry waits while that copy is still on its way to the modem, never goes while the neighbour's receipt is still due, and lets a due beacon go first. Warm-up beacons never go more than two minutes apart, even with a direct message's receipt pending. Receipts are capped: a relay carries a far member's receipt twice at most, a station's own receipt goes once plus once per retry heard, and a far member's receipt walks home a retry interval apart. A gateway takes its beacon back once a mail starts arriving, right up to the moment the beacon reaches the modem. The queue count drops a line once its last copy has gone.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.97

          
Sun, 19 Oct 2026

        

        
The queue count in the status bar counts only what the station still has to key, so it returns to 0 once traffic stops; before, every beacon heard added one. A neighbour hears a station's first direct message and invite first time after a fresh start: a new roster replaces the one before it instead of stacking, a line's retry timer counts from when its copy reaches the modem, and priority retries wait out the modem's 30 s hold rather than keying twice a few seconds apart. Warm-up beacons keep their pace with the station's own lines queued: a due beacon goes ahead of queued invites and lines, waits out one exchange at most, and is taken back from the air queue once at most.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.96

          
Sun, 18 Oct 2026

        

        
A big mail sent just after start reaches the gateway again: a gateway that missed the first chunk, the only one that names the sender, is now asked with that chunk when nothing came back, and every mail chunk a gateway ignores is logged. A receipt waits until the sender has stopped keying, so a station next to the sender is heard, and stations on a busy channel no longer key in step over each other's lines. Warm-up beacons go ahead of a station's own queued lines, and a quiet channel's beacons stay at least 5 minutes apart.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.95

          
Sat, 17 Oct 2026

        

        
Beacons keep their pace on a busy chain: a station sends its first three about a minute apart, then one every 5 minutes, and a beacon that falls due waits until a line's receipt or a mail's acceptance has come instead of landing between them. The beacon countdown shows pending until the beacon reaches the modem. Lines sent just after start wait until another station has been heard. A relay no longer sends a receipt it carries again because another copy of it came in, and a receipt queued behind other frames keys straight after them instead of being held by the modem's carrier sense.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.94

          
Sat, 17 Oct 2026

        

        
Beacons go out on a channel that always reads busy: a station beacons at least every 10 minutes, and a busy reading holds a beacon only until 10 seconds after the last frame it decoded. Each receipt is logged as it goes to the modem, and the beacon log now shows every beacon sent. A modem no longer drops an invite's first copy when a shorter message switches it to a narrower mode, and a relay keeps its copy of a direct message when it hears the sender's retry. A relay that knows only the station that passed it a group line still sends the line on once, even if that station's receipt arrives before its copy goes out.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.93

          
Fri, 16 Oct 2026

        

        
Big mail goes out in one burst again: a chat receipt no longer switches the modem under it, and the gateway waits for the rest of a burst before asking for what is missing. The station next to a sender stops repeating each receipt four or five times, a line a relay carried on stays [rl] until its receipt comes, and beacons are logged as they key. A relay that missed the next station's beacons still passes a direct message's retries on.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.92

          
Fri, 16 Oct 2026

        

        
Idle beacons go every five minutes instead of every minute. A station still beacons about once a minute for its first three minutes, so its neighbours learn it, and beacons still wait ten minutes after traffic, now mail included, and go at least every ten minutes. The beacon countdown shows when the next beacon actually goes.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.91

          
Thu, 15 Oct 2026

        

        
Marks on your line only move forward: no retry or waiting after [rl] or [ok], and the last try waits two intervals before [no]. An invite to a closed group travels the group's 6 hops, so a station four hops out gets it, and anyone who sends on the channel or answers it joins every member list. The ends of a chain relay a line once, a relay keys a line at most three times, and beacons wait ten minutes after traffic.

      

      

        

          
v0.1.90

          
Thu, 15 Oct 2026

        

        
A long radio-plus mail waits for the gateway's acceptance before it tries again, so it reaches Sent instead of keying the whole mail twice. On a chain, each relay keeps sending a group line until it hears the next station, receipts travel back toward the sender only, and neighbours answering the same line take turns. Only stations that heard a #bulletin from its sender ACK it. Beacons pause for a minute after traffic. Receipts carry one new byte; older versions still read them.

Every station on a chain now lists the whole group, group receipts come back from the far end so G1 and G2 reach [all], and every relay keeps sending a bulletin until it hears a station further along. A long radio-plus mail keeps the radio for its whole burst, so the last chunk is no longer lost at 47/48, and a resend keys only the chunks the gateway's newest report says are missing.
