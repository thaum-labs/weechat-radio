docs / email

      
weechat-radio:$ email

      
A second tab next to live chat, with the same mode, frequency, and preset. In wcr gui from v0.1.57 (Send fix in v0.1.59).


      

        
in the app

        
Run wcr update, then open wcr gui. You get an email tab next to live chat and an address at callsign@mail.weechatradio.com. See changelog for the ship notes.

      


      
What it is for

      
Someone on the internet — a club secretary, an event organiser, anyone with normal email — can mail your callsign. You read and send that mail from the desktop app. On the hill you use radio. At home you use the hub. You do not create a Gmail account for WeeChat Radio, and you do not log the app into Gmail.

      
Your on-air identity stays your callsign. Your internet From/To on the wire is callsign@mail.weechatradio.com. Replies stay on that address so they come back to the hub, then to you.


      
The Email tab

      
In wcr gui only (not the TUI or WeeChat in the first version). The left STATION panel stays: mode, frequency, preset. The centre pane switches from live chat to email: inbox, outbox, sent, drafts, compose.

      
New mail puts a badge on email in the top bar. The tray icon can ping, the same way chat does. Guest names that start with ~ do not get an address; Email stays hidden for them.


      
On the live map

      
Email shows as traffic, not a fifth operating mode. Your pin keeps its normal mode colour; a short orange ring marks recent mail activity. When mail went through another station’s gateway, an orange arc links you and that gateway. The map ticker shows MAIL M7TJF -> G0ABC (callsigns only — never the internet address). Hub-only send still lights your pin when mail leaves or arrives at the hub.


      
Your address

      
The first time a licensed-looking callsign opens Email, the hub registers M7TJF@mail.weechatradio.com (your callsign). Tell that address to people on the internet once.

      
Compose To: is an internet mailbox only, such as alex@example.com. Mail to another ham is still live chat.


      
Send

      
      
If the send will key the radio, a confirm lists the destination, that this is third-party traffic, your frequency, preset, and a rough airtime. Internet-only sends skip that modal.

      
Pure radio (not radio-plus) never lets a gateway put your frames on the internet. Internet send is disabled there. Switch to radio-plus or an internet mode. Same idea as chat.


      
Receive — pull, not push

      
      
On radio-plus, Check mail first returns a short list (from, subject, size). You fetch one message, or get all, and confirm each radio step. A session stops around five messages or 20 KB; the rest wait at the hub for the next check.

Frequency and preset stay station-wide, same as chat. A 4 KB mail on hf-deep takes a long time on the air. The confirm exists so you see that before you key.


      
Optional copy to your own mailbox

      
Off by default. In Email settings you can ask the hub to also copy mail to a personal address (for example your usual inbox on a phone or tablet).

      
    
        
  • The hub emails a confirm code to that address. The toggle stays pending until you finish that step.
    • 
        
  • When it is on: mail you send is BCC’d there; mail that arrives at the hub is copied there. Their Reply still waits at the hub for you to sync or Check mail. Copy-to does not steal the Reply off the radio path.
    • 
        
  • Do not auto-forward that mailbox back to callsign@mail.weechatradio.com or you will loop.
    • 
        
  • The personal address never goes over RF. Change this setting while you can still reach the hub.
    • 
      


      
Limits in the first version

      
    
        
  • Plain text only, about 4 KB after HTML is stripped. No attachments.
    • 
        
  • Desktop GUI only.
    • 
        
  • Not a Gmail / Outlook client. The app never stores your Google password.
    • 
        
  • Not Winlink. No @winlink.org, no VARA.
    • 
        
  • Amateur radio: no encryption on the air. Mail on RF is as readable as chat. You are the control operator for third-party traffic to a non-ham.
    •
