weechat-radio:$ Compared with VARA

      
Already on VARA? Here is how WeeChat Radio compares with the VARA FM and VARA HF modems, and with VarAC.


      

        
Who wins at what

        
    
          
  • VARA is faster on a strong, licensed linkWith a paid key, VARA FM Narrow is up to 12,750 bps in about 3 kHz. Wide is up to 25,210 bps on the author’s 4.0 chart (about 6 kHz, and it needs a 9600-baud data port; the current website lists Wide at 19,200 bps). Under the same licence, VARA HF reaches up to 7,050 bps in 2300 Hz Standard, and up to 8,489 bps in 2750 Hz Tactical without compression. WeeChat Radio’s fastest VHF/UHF preset, vhf-fast, is about 2,100–3,200 bps after error correction, and it fits a normal FM voice channel through the microphone socket.
    • 
          
  • WeeChat Radio is the all-in-one appThe modem, live chat, mail, a map, a frequency planner and a Raspberry Pi gateway live in one open-source program. It is free, and it runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and a Pi. You do not need a separate modem or a speed licence.
    • 
          
  • VarAC is the chat layer on the VARA modemIt is a popular, free Windows chat and messaging program by 4Z1AC. It needs VARA (HF or FM) installed beside it, and a paid VARA licence for full speed. WeeChat Radio does not talk to VarAC or Winlink on the air: the protocols are different.
    •

These figures and features come from WeeChat Radio’s repository and from official VARA and VarAC pages, as of 24 Nov 2026. Apps change. If a row is wrong, tell us on GitHub and we will fix it. Sources are at the foot of this page.

What you would gain

        
    
          
  • One program instead of a modem plus a separate chat or mail client.
    • 
          
  • Setup is a short guided walk: run wcr setup, which asks for your callsign, grid and whether you have a radio. To update, click the version at the bottom left of the app and press Update, or run wcr update.
    • 
          
  • The same app on a Mac, a Linux box or a Raspberry Pi, without Wine.
    • 
          
  • A modern, clean interface, in the same indigo and orange look as this site.
    • 
          
  • No paid key for full speed. The source is open.
    • 
          
  • The fastest VHF/UHF preset works through the microphone socket. You do not need a 9600-baud data port.
    • 
          
  • A VR-N76, UV-PRO or similar can run over Bluetooth, with no cable: the radio’s own packet TNC, or modem73 audio over the same link. The VR-N76 / UV-PRO guide covers both. vhf-fast over Bluetooth audio is experimental in v1.0.22, off by default and untested on air.
    • 
          
  • Live group chat, hops between stations that are not all in range, and an internet mode so people with no radio can join.
    • 
          
  • Type /report CALL, or just /report in a direct chat, to ask that station for a signal report. The other station answers on its own with the SNR it heard you at (WeeChat Radio v0.1.109 or later), at most once every 5 minutes. /report auto off stops yours answering. Details: signal reports.
    • 
          
  • Each line shows how far it got: marks such as [tx], [rl] and [ok], plus a retry count when a line is keyed again. In the desktop app, the key button above the chat explains them, and a hover does the same. Lines that reached you by radio also show the SNR, for example -4 dB. The full list is under delivery marks.
    • 
          
  • Your own mail address, a Pi gateway with a browser interface, a range map, a frequency planner and a range test.
    • 
        

      


      

        
What you would give up

        
    
          
  • Raw speed. On a strong, clean FM path, licensed VARA FM is faster than vhf-fast. Licensed VARA HF is also a high-throughput HF modem (up to 8,489 bps in Tactical without compression). That is expected: the VARA family is built for file and email throughput.
    • 
          
  • The Winlink network. If you use VARA today to reach Winlink RMS gateways, WeeChat Radio will not do that. Its mail system is separate.
    • 
          
  • VarAC’s existing users. VarAC reports more than 90,000 operators in over 100 countries, with published calling frequencies and nets. WeeChat Radio cannot join that on-air network.
    • 
          
  • Some VarAC-only features, such as CQ calls and beacons on those shared frequencies, broadcast and relay messaging in the VarAC style, HamPlay, and VARA SAT for QO-100.
    • 
        

        
You do not have to give those things up for good. VARA, VarAC and WeeChat Radio can all stay installed. Use one program on the radio at a time.

      


      

        
Trying it alongside VARA

        
Keep your present VARA or VarAC setup. Install WeeChat Radio from the download page and point it at the same radio and interface: a Digirig, a VOX lead, or a supported Bluetooth handheld. Start with two computers and no radio if you like, or with a local FM test on a quiet channel.

        
Do not let both programs key the radio at once. Stop one before you start the other.

        
vhf-fast is the closest preset to what VARA FM users expect on a strong local link. An experimental Bluetooth-audio option exists in v1.0.22, off by default and untested on air, so a cable or VOX is still the reliable way to try it. Setup asks for your callsign. Nothing on the air is encrypted.

      


      

        
FAQ

        
    
          
  • Can I chat with VarAC or Winlink stations from WeeChat Radio?No. The on-air protocols are different. To reach those networks, use VARA with VarAC or Winlink as you do today.
    • 
          
  • Can I leave VARA installed?Yes. Treat them as two complete setups that share a radio, and run only one at a time.
    • 
          
  • Do I need to pay for full speed?Not for WeeChat Radio. VARA’s unpaid copy is shareware with restrictions; a $75 USD key unlocks full speed. VarAC itself is free, but it still uses that modem.
    • 
          
  • Will vhf-fast work over Bluetooth?Only as an experimental option in v1.0.22, off by default and untested on air. A cabled PTT or a carefully set VOX lead is still the reliable way to run it.
    • 
          
  • Is mail private on the radio?No. WeeChat Radio never encrypts the body on RF, so mail is as readable as chat. That matches amateur-radio rules.
    • 
          
  • Where do the speed figures come from?WeeChat Radio’s numbers are from this project’s own vhf-fast page. VARA FM’s Narrow 12,750 bps and Wide 25,210 bps come from the author’s VARA FM 4.0 features chart; the current VARA website lists Wide at 19,200 bps, so both are shown. VARA HF Standard (2300 Hz) 7,050 bps and Narrow (500 Hz) 1,540 bps come from a 4 June 2020 comment by the author, EA5HVK, on his site. Tactical (2750 Hz) 8,489 bps without compression, and 16,100 bps with compression, come from the current VARA website, which also names the three HF modes.
